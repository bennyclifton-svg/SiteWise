# Billing: Free, Starter and Team plans on Stripe

Status: direction only. No code. Lane C (payments, a new dependency, sign-up
and access). Owner answers of 7 November 2026.

## User outcome

Anyone can sign up and use SiteWise on a capped Free plan. An org owner can
upgrade to a paid plan by card or Australian direct debit, change plan,
update the card, download tax invoices and cancel, without contacting us.
Limits follow the plan at once. Nothing a user made is ever deleted because
of billing.

Why it matters: opening SiteWise to more than a handful of firms is only
safe when each org's Jev reading, storage and seats are bounded by what it
pays for.

## Platform

Stripe, with its hosted pages: Checkout to subscribe and the Customer Portal
for plan changes, card updates, invoices and cancellation. No card data
reaches this server.

Why Stripe over a merchant of record (Paddle, Lemon Squeezy): clients are
Australian firms, Stripe charges in AUD with lower fees, supports BECS direct
debit, and issues tax invoices with our ABN. GST is a fixed 10% tax rate.
Revisit only if overseas sales become material.

## Plans (provisional)

Prices in AUD excluding GST. Caps count per org per calendar month.

| | Free | Starter | Team |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly | 0 | 50 | 200 |
| Annual | 0 | 500 | 2,000 |
| Active projects | 1 | 5 | Unlimited |
| Documents read per month | 50 | 500 | 3,000 |
| Storage | 500 MB | 10 GB | 50 GB |
| Users | 1 | 1 | Up to 5 |

"Documents read" is the user-facing unit for Jev cost: one filed document,
invoice, proposal or program file counts once, whatever readings it gets.
Navigation, typing, cost, procurement and program edits never count, since
they never call Jev.

The caps are guesses. Neither the Jev price nor the Jev cost per document is
recorded in the repo. Step 1 below measures it before prices go live.

Storage check: the first host has 60 GB. Disk grows with paid orgs; alert at
70% use.

## Shape

The org is the billing customer. Its row gains: plan, billing status,
paid-until date, Stripe customer ID, Stripe subscription ID.

1. Sign-up creates an org on Free. Stripe is not called.
2. Upgrade: the server creates a Checkout session tagged with the org ID and
   the chosen price; the user pays on Stripe's page and returns.
3. Stripe sends events to `/api/stripe/webhook`: checkout completed,
   subscription created, updated or deleted, invoice paid, payment failed.
   The handler verifies the Stripe signature, records the event ID so a
   repeat does nothing, and writes the org's plan and status. Stripe is the
   source of truth; the org row is its copy.
4. Manage billing opens a Customer Portal session for the org owner.
5. Limits are checked in Go from the org row at the boundary: create
   project, upload, start a reading, invite a user. No Stripe call happens
   while a user works, so speed budgets are unchanged (the limit check is
   one indexed read inside the existing request).

Failed payment: Stripe retries for about two weeks while the plan stays
active. Then the org falls to Free limits. Over-limit orgs keep full read
access to everything; only new projects, uploads, readings and invites past
the Free caps are refused, with a plain message and an Upgrade button.

Downgrade or cancel: takes effect at the end of the paid period, same rules.

## Recommendation on question 5

Question 5 asks: should anyone on the internet be able to create an account,
now that Free has caps?

Recommend yes, with email verification first. Today sign-up creates a
session at once without checking the address
(`internal/httpapi/accounts.go`). Without verification one person can make
many throwaway Free accounts, each with its own 50 readings. Verification:
the account can sign in at once but cannot upload until the emailed link is
clicked. It reuses the reset-mail path.

## Dependencies

- `github.com/stripe/stripe-go`, Stripe's official library. Widely used and
  maintained by Stripe; the alternative is hand-writing signed API calls and
  webhook verification.
- Team invites. The `memberships` and `invites` tables exist from the
  invite era, but accounts today are one person, one org (`043_accounts.sql`).
  An owner inviting colleagues into their org is its own feature, built
  before Team goes on sale.
- Per-org usage counting (documents read, storage, projects). Needed for the
  caps and for tuning prices.

## Security and edge cases

- The webhook route is exempt from the Origin check and the session check;
  a valid Stripe signature is required instead. Bad or missing signature:
  400 and nothing written.
- Events arrive out of order or twice: apply by Stripe's subscription state
  and event ID, never by arrival order.
- Wrong org: the org ID comes from our own Checkout metadata, never from the
  browser on return. The return page only shows "processing" until the
  webhook lands.
- Only the org owner can open Checkout or the Portal.
- Secrets `SITEWISE_STRIPE_SECRET_KEY` and `SITEWISE_STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET`
  live in `/etc/sitewise/sitewise.env`; production refuses to start without
  them once billing is on.
- Stripe test mode and the Stripe CLI for local webhook forwarding until
  launch.

## Budgets

Webhook handling: p50 ≤ 100 ms, p90 ≤ 250 ms (it is a write path like cost
writes). Limit checks add no round trip to existing paths, which keep their
recorded budgets in `bench/budgets.json`. Checkout and Portal session
creation call Stripe and are not on a hot path; target p90 ≤ 1.5 s.

## Out of scope

Usage-based overage charges, coupons, per-seat pricing beyond the Team cap,
refunds in the app (done in the Stripe dashboard), currencies other than AUD.

## Before taking money

- sitewise.au shows pricing, terms, refund policy, privacy policy and
  contact details (Stripe requires these to activate the account).
- Stripe account activated with ABN and bank details; GST registration
  decided.

## Order

1. Per-org usage counting, then measure Jev cost per document on real use
   and set the caps.
2. Email verification on sign-up.
3. Plan fields, limit checks and over-limit messages (Free only, no Stripe).
4. Stripe Checkout, webhook and Customer Portal for Starter.
5. Team invites, then Team on sale.
6. Public pricing and legal pages, Stripe live mode.

## Done

A Free user upgrades to Starter in test mode, their limits rise within
seconds of the webhook, a failed renewal drops them to Free after the retry
window with all data still readable, and a forged webhook is refused.
