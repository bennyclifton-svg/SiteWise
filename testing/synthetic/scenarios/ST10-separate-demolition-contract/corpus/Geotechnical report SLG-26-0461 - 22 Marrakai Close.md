## Introduction

This report gives the site classification to AS 2870 and footing advice for a new two-storey house
with a slab on ground. We were given the survey by Brennock & Vale Surveyors (BV-26-0144) and the
concept plans by Marlowe Hart Architects.

## Fieldwork

Fieldwork was carried out on 2 April 2026, while the existing house was still standing and occupied.
Three boreholes (BH1 to BH3) were drilled with a small track-mounted rig, clear of the existing house:
BH1 in the front yard and BH2 and BH3 in the back yard. Dynamic cone penetrometer probes were driven
beside each borehole. The boreholes were backfilled with the spoil and the lawn reinstated.

## Subsurface profile

| Depth (m) | Material |
|---|---|
| 0.0 to 0.2 | Topsoil: dark brown sandy silt |
| 0.2 to 0.5 | Fill in BH1 only: brown silty sand with brick fragments |
| 0.5 to 2.3 | Stiff to very stiff grey and orange-brown silty clay, medium plasticity |
| 2.3 to 2.6 | Extremely weathered sandstone, becoming very low strength |
| 2.6 | Auger refusal on sandstone |

No groundwater was encountered in the boreholes to refusal on sandstone at about 2.6 m.

## Laboratory results

Two samples of the silty clay were tested for shrink-swell index. The results were 1.9% and 2.4%.
Moisture contents were close to the plastic limit.

## Site classification

Site classification: Class M in accordance with AS 2870.

The characteristic surface movement is estimated at 25 mm to 30 mm. The footprint of the existing
house could not be investigated while it is standing, and the ground under it may have been cut or
filled when it was built.

## Footings and slab

- A stiffened raft slab designed for Class M is suitable, with edge and internal beams founded in the
  natural stiff clay, below any fill and topsoil.
- Slab on ground at natural level; no excavation near the boundaries.
- No basement is proposed; the house is a slab on ground.
- Allowable bearing pressure in the stiff clay: 100 kPa for beams at least 0.3 m into natural clay.

## Confirmation after demolition

The old brick strip footings along the southern side boundary are to be excavated and removed after
demolition. Any fill under the old house is to be removed to natural clay and backfilled with
compacted fill, or the beams deepened through it. The demolisher's scope should include removing the
old footings and slabs; fill removal and backfill are the builder's earthworks.

We recommend a site visit once the site is cleared to confirm the profile across the house footprint
and the class. If the old footprint shows deeper fill, the slab design should be reviewed.

## Earthworks and site drainage

Strip the topsoil and stockpile it for landscaping. Compact fill in layers not over 200 mm thick to
98% standard maximum dry density. Grade the ground away from the slab edge for at least 1 m, and do
not let water pond against the slab during construction.

## Limitations

The subsurface conditions between the boreholes are inferred. Footing excavations should be
inspected by an engineer before the concrete is placed.
