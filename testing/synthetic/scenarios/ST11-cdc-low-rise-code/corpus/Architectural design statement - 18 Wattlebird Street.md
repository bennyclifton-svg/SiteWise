## Project description

The development comprises an attached dual occupancy of two two-storey townhouses with Torrens title subdivision, with 2 dwellings on proposed Lots 1 and 2. The existing house and garage are to be
demolished. The owners are building a dual occupancy to keep one dwelling and sell the other, so
both dwellings are designed as long-term family homes of the same standard.

Building classification: two Class 1a dwellings, each with an attached Class 10a garage.

Gross floor area: 460 m2 in total. Each dwelling is two storeys.

## Site description and context

The lot is a standard suburban lot with no environmental sensitivities such as bushland, creeks or steep slopes. It has a 17 m frontage to Wattlebird Street and falls gently towards the street.

The two dwellings sit side by side and both
face the street. Each dwelling has its own driveway and vehicle crossing, so the street frontage
reads as two separate entries with landscaped front gardens between them.

## Design

Each dwelling has 4 bedrooms, with three upstairs and a guest bedroom or study on the ground floor.
The ground floor living and dining spaces open to a private rear courtyard, which receives morning
and midday sun. The garages sit under a lower roof that ties into the main roof, which steps the
front elevation down towards the street.

The two dwellings are mirrored in plan but differ in the treatment of the front entries and the
upper-floor windows, so that the pair does not read as identical.

## Separating wall

The dwellings are joined by a fire-rated separating wall on the proposed lot boundary. The separating
wall (the party wall) runs from the slab to the underside of the metal roof. It is designed as an
acoustic wall to achieve Rw + Ctr 50, with discontinuous construction where a wet area adjoins a
habitable room next door.

## Materials

- Face brickwork to the ground floor, in a mid-tone blend
- Lightweight cladding to the upper floor, painted in muted colours
- Metal roof in a light colour
- Powder-coated aluminium windows

## BASIX

The site is in NCC climate zone 5. Each dwelling has heat-pump hot water and is all-electric. The
BASIX commitments include a 3,000 litre rainwater tank for each dwelling, connected to the toilets,
the laundry and the garden taps.

## Trees

No trees on the site are affected by the works; the large gum near the rear boundary is retained well clear of the works. No neighbouring or street trees are affected by the works.

## Planning

Complying development certificate for an attached dual occupancy under the Low Rise Housing Diversity Code: the design meets the code's lot size, frontage and height standards so a certifier can approve it. The subdivision follows under its own complying development certificate once the
dual occupancy is approved.

The parent lot meets the code's minimum lot size and frontage for a dual occupancy. A complying development certificate cannot vary a development standard, so no clause 4.6 variation is involved. The application is determined by a registered certifier, not by council or the local planning panel. No public exhibition or community consultation applies; neighbours receive notice before work starts.
