## 1. Introduction

This report presents the results of a geotechnical investigation for the proposed rear addition and
first-floor addition to the house at 41 Tallowwood Avenue. It provides an AS 2870 site
classification for the addition footprint, records the existing footings under the walls that will
carry the first floor, and gives footing and underpinning recommendations. The work was done in line with our proposal SG-P-0742.

## 2. Fieldwork

Fieldwork was carried out on 9 April 2026 by a geotechnical engineer. It comprised:

- two hand-auger boreholes (BH1 and BH2) within the addition footprint, each to 2.0 m depth
- three test pits (TP1 to TP3) dug by hand against existing footings: TP1 at the rear wall, TP2 at
  the eastern side wall and TP3 at the western side wall, under the rooms the first floor sits over

All three pits found the same detail: the existing walls sit on brick strip footings, about 450 mm
wide, founded on stiff clay at 0.5 m below ground level. No cracking or
undermining of the footings was seen in the pits.

## 3. Subsurface profile

| Depth (m) | Material |
|---|---|
| 0.0 to 0.3 | Fill and topsoil: dark brown silty sand with some brick fragments |
| 0.3 to 2.0 | Stiff to very stiff silty clay, orange-brown and grey, moist |

Groundwater was not encountered in BH1, BH2 or TP1 to TP3 during fieldwork. Groundwater is not expected above the base of the footing excavations, and no dewatering is needed.

## 5. Site classification

Site classification: Class M in accordance with AS 2870.

The characteristic surface movement is estimated at 25 mm. The classification assumes that site
drainage is maintained and that the earthworks are carried out as described below.

## 6. Footing recommendations

No basement is proposed; the addition is a slab on ground. We recommend that the addition footings
be founded in the stiff silty clay at a similar depth to the existing rear wall footing, to limit
differential movement between the old and new parts of the house. An articulation joint should be
provided where the addition meets the existing walls.

No excavation is proposed next to the neighbouring dwellings; the addition footings are shallow strip and pad footings.
An allowable bearing pressure of 100 kPa may be adopted for footings founded in the stiff clay.

## 7. Underpinning of the existing walls

The existing strip footings are narrow and shallow for the added load of the first floor. Where the
structural engineer's check shows they fall short, the walls that carry the first floor should be
underpinned in short, alternate bays. Underpinning founded in the very stiff silty clay at least
0.8 m below ground level may adopt an allowable bearing pressure for underpinning of 150 kPa. Each
bay should be inspected by a geotechnical engineer before concrete is placed.

## 8. Earthworks and site drainage

Strip the fill and topsoil: excavate to a depth of 300 mm to remove topsoil under the addition slab, then proof roll the exposed clay
and replace any soft spots with compacted crushed rock. Fill under the slab should be placed in
layers no thicker than 200 mm and compacted.

Grade the ground away from the new slab, and keep roof water and garden water away from the footings,
in line with CSIRO Building Technology File 18.

## 9. Inspection

We recommend that a geotechnical engineer inspect the footing and underpinning excavations before
concrete is placed.
