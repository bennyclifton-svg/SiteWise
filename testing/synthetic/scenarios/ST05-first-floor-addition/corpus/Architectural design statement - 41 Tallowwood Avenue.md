# Architectural design statement

Kestrel Hill Architects Pty Ltd
Suite 3, 12 Larkspur Lane, Concord NSW 2137
Project: Alterations and additions, 41 Tallowwood Avenue, Concord NSW 2137
Client: Tomas and Leah Ferreira

## Project description

The proposal is a rear addition, a first-floor addition and internal alterations to a brick house of
the late 1950s. The rear skillion and laundry lean-to are removed and replaced with a new kitchen,
living and dining room that opens to the garden. The bathroom and laundry are redone, and two internal walls are
removed to open the hallway and the old kitchen to the new rooms.

New first-floor addition over the existing house containing the main bedroom and ensuite. The house becomes two storeys; the existing roof over the front rooms is unchanged.

- Existing gross floor area: 140 m2
- Added gross floor area: 55 m2
- Proposed gross floor area after the works: 195 m2

Building classification: Class 1a dwelling.

## Site description and context

The site is a standard suburban lot with no environmental sensitivities such as bushland, waterways or
contamination. The street is lined with
brick houses of the 1940s to 1960s, many of them altered and extended at the rear. The house is a
free-standing detached house with side setbacks on both boundaries. The addition is set 1.5 m from both side
boundaries, inside the line of the existing eastern wall, so the 1.0 m side path on the eastern side
is kept.

## Design

The addition will build over the footprint of the demolished skillion and extend it about 3.5 m into
the rear yard. Going up rather than out keeps most of the garden behind. A new paved terrace and a low rear deck connect the
living area to the garden. Wide sliding doors face north-east to the garden, with a deep eave for
summer shading.

The two internal walls between the hallway, the old kitchen and the back room are loadbearing. They
are removed and replaced with steel beams, so the house opens from the front door through to the
garden. The ground-floor addition has a low-pitched skillion roof.

The main bedroom and ensuite sit on a new first floor over the rear rooms of the existing house,
looking over the garden. The existing rear roof is removed for the first floor, and the builder
provides a temporary roof to keep the house dry until the new roof is on. The first floor is set
back behind the front roof, so the street view of the house is unchanged. Side windows upstairs are
highlight windows to protect the neighbours' privacy.

A new timber stair rises from the living area, with balustrades to the stair and the upstairs
landing. The existing brick walls that carry the first floor are underpinned to the structural
engineer's design.

## Materials

- Face brick to match the existing house on the side walls of the addition
- Lightweight cladding, painted, to the rear wall around the sliding doors and to the first floor
- Colorbond roof sheeting to the ground-floor addition and the first floor
- Aluminium-framed double-glazed windows and doors
- Dulux Heritage colour range to be used for the external paintwork

## Wet areas

The bathroom and laundry are rebuilt with new waterproofing to AS 3740. Flood test the shower recess
before tiling. The laundry moves next to the back door.

## Trees

No trees within 5 m of the works; no trees on the site are affected by the works. No neighbouring or
street trees are affected by the works. No tree removal or pruning is proposed.

## Services

The existing underground supply has the capacity for the new loads, including the heat-pump hot water
and air-conditioning. The gas cooktop stays on the existing gas supply. Roof water from the addition
drains to the existing system.

## Energy

The site is in Climate zone 5. The BASIX certificate is based on the addition's insulation, glazing
and shading, with heat-pump hot water and LED lighting throughout.

## Planning

The design complies with the height and floor space ratio standards, so no clause 4.6 variation is
sought. The application will be determined by council staff under delegation, not the local planning
panel. No public exhibition or community consultation is proposed; council will notify the
neighbours in the usual way.
