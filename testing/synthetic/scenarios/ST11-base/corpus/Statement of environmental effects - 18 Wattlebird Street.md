## 1. Introduction

This statement accompanies a development application to the City of Parramatta for demolition, an attached dual occupancy and Torrens title subdivision at 18 Wattlebird Street, Carlingford NSW 2118. It
describes the site and the proposal and assesses the proposal under section 4.15 of the
Environmental Planning and Assessment Act 1979. It should be read with the architectural drawings by
Fernleigh Architects, the stormwater concept plan by Merriden Civil Engineering and the draft plan of
subdivision.

## 2. Site and locality

The site is a rectangular lot of about 700 m2 with a 17 m frontage to Wattlebird Street. It falls
gently towards the street. The existing building on the site, a single-storey brick dwelling, is to be demolished, together with the detached garage, paths and driveway.

The surrounding area is low density residential, with detached dwellings and dual occupancies on
lots of a similar size.

## 3. The proposal

The application seeks consent for:

- demolition of the existing dwelling, garage, paths and driveway
- construction of an attached dual occupancy, with 2 dwellings on proposed Lots 1 and 2, each two
  storeys with 4 bedrooms and an attached garage
- a separate driveway and vehicle crossing for each dwelling
- stormwater drainage with on-site detention
- Torrens title subdivision of the lot along the line between the two dwellings

Gross floor area 460 m2.

## 4. Planning controls

### Zoning and permissibility

The site is zoned R2 Low Density Residential under Parramatta Local Environmental Plan 2023. A dual
occupancy is permitted with consent in the zone, and so is subdivision.

### Minimum lot size and frontage

Both proposed lots meet the LEP minimum lot size and frontage standards for subdivision of a dual
occupancy, and the parent lot meets the minimum for the dual occupancy itself. The proposal complies with the LEP height of buildings and floor space ratio standards. No clause 4.6 variation is required.

### Development control plan

| DCP control | Proposal | Complies |
|---|---|---|
| Front setback | 6.0 m, in line with the neighbouring dwellings | Yes |
| Side setbacks | 0.9 m ground floor, 1.5 m upper floor | Yes |
| Landscaped area | Landscaped front gardens and rear courtyards to both dwellings | Yes |
| Private open space | Rear courtyard to each dwelling, directly off the living area | Yes |
| Car parking | One garage per dwelling with a separate driveway | Yes |
| Solar access | Rear courtyards and neighbouring living areas keep winter sun (shadow diagrams) | Yes |
| Visual privacy | Upper-floor side windows are high-sill or obscure glazed | Yes |

## 5. Assessment under section 4.15

### Built form and streetscape

The pair of dwellings presents as two separate entries to the street, with varied front entries,
face brickwork to the ground floor and lighter cladding above. The garages sit under a lower roof
that steps the front elevation down.

### Privacy and overshadowing

Upper-floor side windows are high-sill or obscure glazed. The shadow diagrams show that the
neighbouring rear yards receive at least three hours of sun between 9 am and 3 pm in mid-winter.

### Stormwater

Council's stormwater management policy requires on-site detention for the dual occupancy. A tank is
provided under the Lot 1 driveway, discharging by gravity to the kerb, to the civil engineer's
concept design.

### Demolition and waste

A demolition and waste management plan accompanies the application. A hazardous materials survey has
been carried out, and any asbestos found will be removed by a licensed removalist before demolition.

### Hazards

The land is not a heritage item and is not within a heritage conservation area. The land is not identified as bush fire prone land on the planning certificate. The land is not subject to flood related development controls.

## 6. Determination and notification

Determination under delegation; no local planning panel referral is expected. Neighbour notification under council's community participation plan; no public exhibition.

## 7. Conclusion

The proposal is permissible in the R2 zone, meets the LEP lot size and frontage standards and the
DCP controls, and has acceptable impacts on the neighbours and the street. We recommend that consent
be granted, subject to reasonable conditions.
