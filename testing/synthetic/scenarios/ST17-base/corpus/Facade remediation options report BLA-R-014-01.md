# Facade remediation options report

Brightline Architects Pty Ltd, with input from Quoin Cost Consultants, Kestrel Fire Engineering,
Mullion Facade Engineering and Tallow Consulting Engineers

Project: Tideway Apartments, 27 Tideway Crescent, Meadowbank NSW 2114
Prepared for: the strata committee and the general meeting of the owners corporation

## Purpose

This report sets out options for the remediation of the combustible cladding on the existing building,
compares them on compliance, appearance, cost and disruption to residents, and recommends one for the
owners corporation to adopt at its general meeting. Revision 1 adds the quantity surveyor's order of
cost and the structural engineer's slab-edge findings.

## Building

Tideway Apartments is an 8-storey residential flat building of 56 apartments, constructed in 2011, with
a reinforced concrete frame. It is an existing building, occupied throughout, and it stands on the
street boundary on both frontages.

## Findings summary

- The 2025 audit found PE-core aluminium composite panels on the balcony spandrels and blades on all
  four elevations, an EPS render system on the level 7 and 8 setback walls, and combustible foil
  sarking behind the panels.
- The structural engineer's sample openings found sound slab edges, and every pull-out test passed.
- Windows are retained and protected throughout.
- Balconies have glass balustrades, which are retained.

## Options

**Option 1: ACP replacement on a direct fix.** Replace the PE-core panels with solid aluminium panels
fixed directly to the existing support framing, and re-render the setback walls on a non-combustible
board. Cheapest and quickest, but the existing framing and the lack of a drained cavity limit the
warranty, and the appearance barely changes.

**Option 2: ventilated rainscreen (recommended).** The replacement is a ventilated rainscreen cladding
system of solid aluminium panels designed by the facade engineer, on a new aluminium subframe fixed to
the slab edges, with mineral wool insulation and non-combustible sarking behind a drained and
ventilated cavity. The setback walls receive the same system. It gives the best long-term performance
and the clearest compliance path, and it lets the building's appearance be refreshed.

**Option 3: fibre cement rainscreen.** A rainscreen of fibre cement panels on a similar subframe. Lower
material cost than Option 2, but more joints, more maintenance and a weaker appearance at this height.

| | Option 1 | Option 2 | Option 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compliance path | Deemed-to-satisfy | Deemed-to-satisfy | Deemed-to-satisfy |
| Weathertightness | Fair | Best | Good |
| Appearance | Little change | Refreshed | Refreshed |
| Time on site | 30 weeks | 36 weeks | 36 weeks |
| Relative cost | Lowest | Highest | Middle |

## Order of cost

Quoin Cost Consultants have prepared the order of cost for each option, including the scaffold and
hoarding, the builder's preliminaries and contingencies. Option 2 costs more than the other two, but
the committee preferred its performance and appearance when the options were presented.

Estimated construction cost for the recommended option: $6,850,000 excluding GST. We recommend the
owners corporation adopts this as its construction budget.

The owners corporation is funding the works through special levies without government assistance.

## Approvals

- A development application is required for the change to the external materials and appearance.
- The remediation is building work under the Design and Building Practitioners Act 2020; regulated
  designs and design compliance declarations are required.

The following matters were checked with the consultant team and do not apply to this project:

- The site is not a heritage item and is not within a heritage conservation area.
- The proposal does not change the height or floor space ratio and needs no clause 4.6 variation.
- It is not regionally significant development and will not be determined by a planning panel.
- No community consultation is planned beyond the owners corporation's own resident meetings.
- No connecting with Country or Aboriginal cultural heritage input is required for a recladding.
- No Green Star, NABERS or net zero target applies.
- BASIX does not apply because the works are to common property.
- The site has a single residential flat building; there are no multiple buildings.
- The works do not build over or next to any sewer main or water main.
- The existing building stormwater system, which has no on-site detention, is not altered.
- HBCF cover is not required because the building has a rise of more than three storeys.
- No claim is being pursued.

## Delivery

The consultant team will fully document the works for a lump sum tender; traditional procurement. The
building contract will be AS 4000-1997 General conditions of contract, with special conditions, let as
a lump sum contract to a remedial builder.

All combustible cladding and insulation will be removed and replaced to the deemed-to-satisfy
provisions.

The building is on the street boundary on both frontages: the perimeter scaffold and an overhead
hoarding stand over the council footpath. A full perimeter scaffold is used. On the rear, scaffold base
plates sit on the podium slab over the basement car park. All elevations face the street or common
property.

There will be no staged delivery: all elevations will be completed in one continuous stage.

## Recommendation

We recommend that the owners corporation adopts Option 2, the ventilated rainscreen of solid aluminium
panels, adopts its order of cost as the construction budget, and instructs the consultant team to
proceed to the development application and documentation.
