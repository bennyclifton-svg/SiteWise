## Project description

The development comprises multi dwelling housing comprising 4 two-storey townhouses in one attached row, with community title subdivision into 4 lots with a common driveway, giving 4 dwellings on proposed Lots 1 to 4 with the driveway held as association property. The existing house and garage are to be
demolished. The owners are building a row of 4 townhouses to keep one and sell the others, so
every townhouse is designed as a long-term home of the same standard.

Building classification: four Class 1a dwellings, each with an attached Class 10a garage.

Gross floor area: 460 m2 in total. Each townhouse is two storeys.

## Site description and context

The lot is a standard suburban lot with no environmental sensitivities such as bushland, creeks or steep slopes. It has a 17 m frontage to Wattlebird Street and falls gently towards the street.

One common driveway runs along the southern boundary from a single vehicle crossing. The townhouses
face the driveway in one row, with the end townhouse presenting a designed elevation to the street.
The bin store and the mailboxes sit at the front of the site, beside the driveway entry.

## Design

Each townhouse has 2 bedrooms upstairs. The ground floor living and dining spaces open to a private
rear courtyard, which receives morning and midday sun. Each single garage is entered off the common
driveway and sits under a lower roof that ties into the main roof, which breaks down the length of
the row.

Entries, window groupings and the cladding colour vary along the row so that it does not read as a
single repeated unit.

## Separating walls

The townhouses are joined by three fire-rated separating walls, each on a proposed lot boundary. Each
separating wall (a party wall) runs from the slab to the underside of the metal roof. They are
designed as acoustic walls to achieve Rw + Ctr 50, with discontinuous construction where a wet area
adjoins a habitable room next door.

## Materials

- Face brickwork to the ground floor, in a mid-tone blend
- Lightweight cladding to the upper floor, painted in muted colours
- Metal roof in a light colour
- Powder-coated aluminium windows

## BASIX

The site is in NCC climate zone 5. Each townhouse has heat-pump hot water and is all-electric. The
BASIX commitments include a 2,000 litre rainwater tank for each townhouse, connected to the toilets,
the laundry and the garden taps.

## Trees

No trees on the site are affected by the works; the large gum in the neighbouring rear yard is well clear of Townhouse 4. No neighbouring or street trees are affected by the works.

## Planning

Townhouses of this kind can be complying development under the low-rise housing code where every
standard is met. The design does not meet the code's landscaped area standard, so a DA will be lodged with the City of Parramatta, and the design is assessed against
council's LEP and DCP.

The development meets the minimum lot size and frontage standards for multi dwelling housing, so no clause 4.6 variation is sought. The application will be determined by council staff under delegation, not the local planning panel. No public exhibition or community consultation is proposed beyond neighbour notification.
