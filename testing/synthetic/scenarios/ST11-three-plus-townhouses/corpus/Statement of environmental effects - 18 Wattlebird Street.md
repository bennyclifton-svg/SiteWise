## 1. Introduction

This statement accompanies a development application to the City of Parramatta for demolition, multi dwelling housing comprising 4 townhouses and community title subdivision at 18 Wattlebird Street, Carlingford NSW 2118. It
describes the site and the proposal and assesses the proposal under section 4.15 of the
Environmental Planning and Assessment Act 1979. It should be read with the architectural drawings by
Fernleigh Architects, the stormwater concept plan by Merriden Civil Engineering, the traffic and
parking report by Wayfinder Traffic Engineering, the waste management plan by Binley Waste
Consulting and the draft community plan.

## 2. Site and locality

The site is a rectangular lot of about 700 m2 with a 17 m frontage to Wattlebird Street. It falls
gently towards the street. The existing building on the site, a single-storey brick dwelling, is to be demolished, together with the detached garage, paths and driveway.

The surrounding area is low density residential, with detached dwellings and some attached housing
on lots of a similar size.

## 3. The proposal

The application seeks consent for:

- demolition of the existing dwelling, garage, paths and driveway
- construction of multi dwelling housing in one attached row, with 4 dwellings on proposed Lots 1 to 4,
  each two storeys with 2 bedrooms and a single garage
- a common driveway along the southern boundary with a single vehicle crossing
- a bin store and mailboxes at the front of the site
- stormwater drainage with on-site detention
- community title subdivision into proposed Lots 1 to 4 and association property

Gross floor area 460 m2.

## 4. Planning controls

### Zoning and permissibility

The site is zoned R2 Low Density Residential under Parramatta Local Environmental Plan 2023. Multi
dwelling housing of this kind is permissible in the zone under the Housing SEPP's low-rise housing
provisions, and community title subdivision is permitted with consent.

### Minimum lot size and frontage

The parent lot meets the minimum lot size and frontage standards that apply to multi dwelling
housing, and the proposed lots are consistent with the community title provisions. The proposal complies with the height of buildings and floor space ratio standards. No clause 4.6 variation is required.

### Development control plan

## 5. Assessment under section 4.15

### Built form and streetscape

The row presents a designed end elevation to the street, with the common driveway and a landscaped
front garden beside it. Entries, window groupings and cladding colours vary along the row. Face
brickwork to the ground floor and lighter cladding above keep the scale domestic.

### Traffic and parking

The traffic and parking report confirms that one space per townhouse meets the parking rate, that
cars can enter and leave the site in a forward direction, and that the small number of extra trips
has no noticeable effect on Wattlebird Street.

### Waste

The waste management plan covers demolition, construction and ongoing collection. Bins for every
townhouse are kept in the bin store behind the front fence line and wheeled to the kerb for
council's kerbside collection.

### Privacy and overshadowing

Upper-floor windows facing the neighbours are high-sill or obscure glazed. The shadow diagrams show
that the neighbouring rear yards receive at least three hours of sun between 9 am and 3 pm in
mid-winter.

### Stormwater

Council's stormwater management policy requires on-site detention for the multi dwelling housing. A
tank is provided under the common driveway, discharging by gravity to the kerb, to the civil
engineer's concept design.

### Demolition and waste

A demolition and waste management plan accompanies the application. A hazardous materials survey has
been carried out, and any asbestos found will be removed by a licensed removalist before demolition.

### Hazards

The land is not a heritage item and is not within a heritage conservation area. The land is not identified as bush fire prone land on the planning certificate. The land is not subject to flood related development controls.

## 6. Determination and notification

Determination under delegation; no local planning panel referral is expected. Neighbour notification under council's community participation plan; no public exhibition.

## 7. Conclusion

The proposal is permissible in the R2 zone, meets the lot size and frontage standards and the
DCP controls, and has acceptable impacts on the neighbours and the street. We recommend that consent
be granted, subject to reasonable conditions.
