## 1. Summary

Two trees are affected by the proposal. Both are healthy, both are worth keeping, and both can be
retained with the design changes and protection measures in this report. All trees are kept.

- The rear slab edge is within the structural root zone of the blue gum (Tree 1); a 22% TPZ encroachment.
  Under AS 4970 this is a major encroachment, so the rear edge must be redesigned to bridge the roots.
- The driveway cut encroaches into the tree protection zone of the neighbour's jacaranda (Tree 2, No. 25).
  The encroachment is minor and is acceptable with hand digging and root cutting under supervision.

## 2. Method

The trees were inspected from ground level on 13 May 2026 using visual tree assessment (VTA). Trunk
diameters were measured at 1.4 m above ground (DBH) and just above the root buttress. The tree
protection zone (TPZ) and structural root zone (SRZ) were calculated under AS 4970-2009 Protection of
trees on development sites. Encroachments were measured against Harbourline Architects' site plan
A-101 Rev A and the detail survey by Calder & Moss Surveyors. The jacaranda was assessed from the
client's side of the boundary fence.

## 5. Encroachment assessment

**Tree 1, blue gum.** The house, the covered terrace and the rear terrace slab together encroach into
about 22% of the TPZ. The rear edge of the terrace slab comes to 3.3 m from the trunk, inside the
3.9 m SRZ. A conventional slab edge beam here would sever structural roots and is not supported.
Because the encroachment exceeds 10% and reaches the SRZ, AS 4970 requires the arborist to show the
tree will remain viable. In our opinion it will, provided the changes in section 6 are made. The
tree has a broad, even crown, no sign of stress, and open lawn on the remaining sides for root
growth.

**Tree 2, jacaranda.** The driveway is on the northern side of the front setback, 1.8 m from the
boundary, opposite the jacaranda. Its cut, up to about 400 mm below existing ground near the garage,
enters the TPZ over a length of about 7.5 m and a width of up to 1.6 m. This is about 9% of the TPZ
and outside the SRZ, so it is a minor encroachment under AS 4970. Roots of 25 to 50 mm diameter are
likely in the cut and should be cut cleanly rather than torn by machinery.

## 6. Design changes recommended

1. Rear terrace: carry the last 1.5 m of the terrace slab on bored piers with a suspended beam at or
   above existing ground level, so that no trench is dug inside the SRZ. Pier positions to be set out
   with the arborist on site and moved to avoid any root over 50 mm.
2. Driveway: dig the cut by hand where it is inside the jacaranda's TPZ. Roots under 50 mm met in the hand dig are
   cut cleanly with a sharp saw by the project arborist; larger roots are retained and reported before
   work continues.
3. Services: route new underground services along the southern side of the lot, outside both TPZs.
4. No change of level, stockpiling, wash-out or parking within either TPZ outside the approved
   work areas.

These changes have been discussed with Priya Ramanathan of Harbourline Architects and will be shown
on the DA drawings.

## 7. Tree protection plan

- Protective fencing: 1.8 m temporary mesh panels on the TPZ line of the blue gum, set back where the
  approved work enters the zone, before any site work starts
- Ground protection: geotextile under 100 mm of mulch, with rumble boards where access is needed
  inside the blue gum's TPZ
- Trunk protection: timber battens strapped over padding on the blue gum to 2 m
- Signs on the fencing identifying the tree protection zone
- The neighbour's jacaranda is protected by the existing boundary fence, which stays in place
  during the driveway work

## 8. Hold points

| Hold point | When | Action |
|---|---|---|
| HP1 | Before site establishment | Project arborist inspects and certifies the protection fencing and ground protection |
| HP2 | During the driveway cut and the terrace pier holes | Project arborist supervises the digging inside the TPZs and cuts roots cleanly |

Work inside a TPZ must not start until the hold point has been released in writing. A compliance
certificate will be issued after each visit and a copy given to the certifier.

## 9. Conclusion

With the suspended rear edge, hand digging at the driveway and the protection measures above, both
trees can be retained in good condition. We support the application on arboricultural grounds.
