## Introduction

This report presents the results of a geotechnical investigation for the proposed mixed-use
development at 40-44 Halloran Street. The proposal includes two basement levels, bulk excavation to
about 7 m below Halloran Street. The investigation was carried out in accordance with our proposal
BRG-P-0412.

## Fieldwork

Fieldwork was carried out on 7 to 9 April 2026. Four boreholes (BH1 to BH4) were drilled with a
track-mounted rig to 12.5 m depth: BH1 and BH2 through the rear car park of
Nos. 42 and 44, BH3 and BH4 in the rear yard of No. 40. Soil was sampled and tested by standard penetration testing, and rock was cored with NMLC
coring. Boreholes were backfilled and the asphalt patched on completion.

## Subsurface profile

| Unit | Description | Depth to top (m) |
|---|---|---|
| Fill | Sandy clay with brick and asphalt fragments | 0.0 |
| Residual clay | Stiff to very stiff silty clay, high plasticity | 0.3 to 0.6 |
| Weathered shale | Extremely to highly weathered shale, very low to low strength | about 3.0 |
| Ashfield Shale | Moderately weathered to fresh shale, medium strength | about 6.0 |

No groundwater was encountered to the base of the boreholes; seepage only at the clay and shale
contact, which a drained basement handles. Seepage flows are expected to be small and can be collected
by a drainage layer behind the shotcrete and pumped to the stormwater system.

## Excavation and adjoining properties

Bulk excavation to the eastern boundary adjoining the two-storey shop at No. 38 will extend into the
medium strength shale. The adjoining shop at No. 38 is founded on shale at shallow depth; no
underpinning is needed. The three-storey building at No. 46 to the west was inspected from the
footpath only; its footings should be confirmed by test pits before retention design is finalised.

The weathered shale can be excavated with large excavators; the medium strength shale will need
ripping and rock hammers or rock saws near the boundaries.

## Retention

We recommend a soldier pile wall with shotcrete infill between the piles, supported by temporary
ground anchors during excavation until the basement slabs are built. Piles should be socketed at least
1.5 m into medium strength shale below the bulk excavation level. Design parameters are given in
Table 3 of the appendix. Anchors extending under neighbouring land require the owners' consent.

## Footings

Pad footings founded on medium strength shale at the base of the excavation may be designed for an
allowable bearing pressure of 1,500 kPa, subject to inspection by a geotechnical engineer.

## Spoil

All spoil is to be classified before disposal in line with the NSW EPA Waste Classification
Guidelines. Natural shale is likely to be classed as virgin excavated natural material.

## Vibration

Rock hammering near the boundaries is to be kept within vibration limits for the neighbours, with
monitoring on the adjoining properties during bulk excavation.
