## Introduction

Halcyon Eastwood Pty Ltd proposes to redevelop the site for a mixed-use development with ground-floor
retail and apartments above. Because the proposal changes the use of part of the land from commercial
to residential, the consent authority must consider whether the land is suitable for that use. This
preliminary site investigation (PSI) was commissioned to inform that consideration. It follows the
NSW EPA Consultants Reporting on Contaminated Land guidelines and the National Environment Protection
(Assessment of Site Contamination) Measure.

## Site history

We reviewed a historical title search, aerial photographs from 1943 to 2024 and council's property
records.

- In the 1943 and 1951 photographs the site is a single dwelling with open yards.
- The rear part of No. 40, now the grassed yard behind the dwelling, was a service station from the
  mid-1950s until 1992. Two fuel bowsers and a canopy are visible in the photographs from 1955 to
  1991. Council's file holds the original approval for the service station but no record of the
  removal of its underground tanks, and none was found in the title documents.
- The shops on Nos. 42 and 44 and the present dwelling on No. 40 appear by 1965 and are unchanged in
  form in every later photograph.
- A dry cleaner operated in the ground-floor shop at No. 42 from 1971 to 2004, according to council
  and title records.
- The other uses over that time were a newsagent and a bakery in the shops, and the dwelling on
  No. 40.

## Site inspection

Hannah Wu inspected the site on 2 March 2026. The shops were trading and the dwelling occupied. In the
rear yard of No. 40 there is a cut-off vent pipe stub against the rear fence and a rectangular
concrete slab of about 6 m by 3 m in the grass, which is consistent with a former tank pit or fill point. No odours
were noted inside the shops, and there was no staining or stockpiles elsewhere on the site.

## Limited sampling

Six shallow test pits were dug in the rear yard of No. 40 and through the car park behind the shops to a maximum depth of 1.2 m. Soil
samples were analysed for heavy metals, hydrocarbons, BTEX, PAHs and OCPs, and fill samples were
screened for asbestos.

Total recoverable hydrocarbons and BTEX exceeded the residential criteria (HSL A) of the NEPM in the
two pits nearest the concrete slab. The other four pits were below the residential criteria (HIL A and
HSL A). No asbestos was detected in the fill. The fill is a thin layer of sandy clay with brick
fragments over natural clay, with a hydrocarbon odour at 0.9 m in the two pits near the former tanks.

No samples were taken inside the shop at No. 42; chlorinated solvents from the former dry cleaner
cannot be assessed without soil vapour sampling under the slab.

## Conclusions

The site was a service station until 1992; underground tanks may remain. A dry cleaner operated in the ground-floor shop at No. 42. The PSI recommends a detailed site investigation to define both.

The land is potentially contaminated. The limited sampling confirms hydrocarbon impact near the former
tanks, and the former dry cleaner is a potential source of chlorinated solvents.

We recommend:

1. A detailed site investigation, with soil bores across the former service station area, a search
   for remaining underground tanks, and soil vapour sampling around the former dry cleaner at No. 42.
2. A remediation action plan, prepared from the results of the detailed site investigation.
3. Engagement of a NSW EPA accredited site auditor before the development application is determined,
   so that a site audit statement can confirm the land will be suitable for residential use after
   remediation and validation.

Spoil from the basement excavation is to be classified before disposal in line with the NSW EPA Waste
Classification Guidelines. The shops and dwelling date from the 1960s. A hazardous building materials
survey should be carried out before demolition; it is outside the scope of this PSI.
