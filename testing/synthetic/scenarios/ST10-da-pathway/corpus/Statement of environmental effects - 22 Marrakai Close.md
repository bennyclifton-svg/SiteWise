## Introduction

This statement accompanies a development application to Sutherland Shire Council for demolition of
the existing dwelling and construction of a new two-storey dwelling. The second storey exceeds the
Housing Code side setback, so complying development is not available.

The statement describes the site and the proposal, assesses it against the planning controls and the
matters for consideration under section 4.15 of the Environmental Planning and Assessment Act 1979,
and concludes that the application can be approved.

## The site

The site is 22 Marrakai Close, Caringbah South NSW 2229, a rectangular lot of 619.8 m2 with a 15.85 m
frontage to the street. It falls about 1.1 m from the street to the rear. The lot holds a 1962
single dwelling, a detached garage and a shed. The street is lined with detached houses of mixed age,
several of them recent two-storey homes.

The land is zoned R2 Low Density Residential under the Sutherland Shire Local Environmental Plan 2015.
The land is not a heritage item and is not within a heritage conservation area. The land is not
identified as bush fire prone land on the planning certificate. The land is not subject to flood
related development controls.

## The proposal

- Demolition of the existing dwelling, detached garage, shed, slabs, paths and driveway
- Construction of a two-storey dwelling of 302 m2 gross floor area with four bedrooms and an attached
  double garage
- A new driveway and vehicle crossing at the northern end of the frontage
- Landscaping to the front and rear gardens, and a 5,000 L rainwater tank

## Why a development application

The upper floor is set 1.2 m from the northern side boundary. The Housing Code requires a greater
side setback for that part of the wall, so the dwelling is not complying development and the
application is made to council. Demolition is included in the same application.

## Planning controls

| Control | Standard | Proposal | Complies |
|---|---|---|---|
| LEP height of buildings | 8.5 m | 7.6 m | Yes |
| LEP floor space ratio | 0.55:1 | 0.49:1 | Yes |
| DCP front setback | 7.0 m or the average of neighbours | 7.0 m | Yes |
| DCP side setback, ground floor | 0.9 m | 1.2 m north, 1.5 m south | Yes |
| DCP side setback, upper floor | 1.5 m | 1.2 m north, 1.5 m south | No, north side (see below) |
| DCP rear setback | 6.0 m | 12.5 m | Yes |
| DCP landscaped area | 35% | 43% | Yes |

The proposal meets the height and floor space ratio standards in the LEP. No clause 4.6 variation is
required.

**Upper floor side setback.** The northern upper floor wall is 300 mm closer to the boundary than the
DCP control. The objectives of the control are met: the wall faces north, so it casts no additional
shadow on the neighbour to the north; the upper floor windows on that side are highlight
windows with sills at 1.7 m, so there is no overlooking; and the wall is broken by a recessed stair
bay, so it does not read as a long unbroken wall from the street or the neighbour's yard.

## Section 4.15 matters

**Context and setting.** A two-storey house of this size is consistent with the recent houses in the
street and the R2 zone objectives.

**Privacy.** Living areas are on the ground floor and open to the rear garden. Upper floor bedroom
windows face the street and the rear; side windows upstairs are highlight windows or obscure glazed.

**Overshadowing.** Shadow diagrams for 21 June show the neighbour to the south keeps at least
three hours of sun to its rear living area and half of its private open space between 9 am and 3 pm.

**Demolition and waste management.** A waste management plan accompanies the application. Brick,
concrete and metal are separated on site for recycling. The builder carries out demolition under
AS 2601 with erosion and sediment controls in place from the first day.

**Hazardous materials.** A demolition hazardous materials survey has been carried out by Clearfield
Hazmat Services (report CHS-26-0733). The bonded asbestos sheeting it identifies is removed by a
licensed removalist before demolition, and demolition starts only after a clearance certificate is
issued.

**Stormwater.** Roof and surface water drains by gravity to the kerb in Marrakai Close, as shown on
the stormwater concept plan by Drummond Civil. The rainwater tank collects roof water for the
toilets, laundry and garden.

**Landscape.** The landscape plan by Fernleaf Landscape Architects provides 43% landscaped area,
with deep soil planting in the front and rear gardens and a planting schedule of mostly local native
species.

**Energy and water.** A BASIX certificate for the new dwelling accompanies the application. The house
is all-electric.

**Public interest.** Determination under delegation; the application does not need referral to the
local planning panel.
No community consultation is required beyond council's standard neighbour notification; no public
exhibition.

## Conclusion

The proposal is a well-designed family house that meets the LEP standards and, apart from the
northern upper floor side setback, the DCP controls. The variation causes no shadow, privacy or bulk
impact on the neighbour. We recommend that council grant consent, subject to appropriate conditions.
