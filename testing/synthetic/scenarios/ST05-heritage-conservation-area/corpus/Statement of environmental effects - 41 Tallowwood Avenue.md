# Statement of environmental effects

Prepared by Carrick Planning Pty Ltd for Tomas and Leah Ferreira, with Kestrel Hill Architects
Site: 41 Tallowwood Avenue, Concord NSW 2137 (Lot 23 DP 30418)

## 1. Introduction

This statement accompanies a development application to the City of Canada Bay. The site is within a heritage conservation area, so complying development is not available.
It describes the site and the proposal and assesses the likely impacts under section 4.15 of the
Environmental Planning and Assessment Act 1979. It should be read with the architectural drawings,
the heritage impact statement, the survey, the stormwater concept plan and the BASIX certificate.

## 2. The site

The site is 41 Tallowwood Avenue, Concord NSW 2137, a rectangular lot with a 12.2 m frontage, on the
northern side of the street. It holds a detached brick house with a hipped tiled roof, built on a
lot left vacant when the surrounding Federation houses were built. Council's records hold the 1958 building approval and the 1987 approval for the rear skillion and laundry lean-to.

## 3. The proposal

The proposal is alterations and additions to an existing building, a brick dwelling. It comprises:

- demolition of the rear skillion and laundry lean-to
- a new rear addition containing a kitchen, living and dining room
- a paved terrace and low rear deck
- internal alterations: the bathroom and laundry rebuilt, and two internal walls removed

Existing gross floor area 140 m2. Added gross floor area 55 m2. The addition is single storey, with a
low skillion roof below the eave of the main roof.

## 4. Planning controls

## 5. Environmental effects

### Privacy and overshadowing

The addition is single level with a floor close to ground level. Side windows are highlight windows
or face the side fences, so there is no overlooking of the adjoining rear yards. The shadow diagrams
show that the adjoining houses keep at least three hours of sun to their living rooms and private
open space between 9 am and 3 pm at the winter solstice.

### Stormwater

Roof water from the addition and the terrace connects to the existing system, which drains by gravity
to the kerb in Tallowwood Avenue. A stormwater concept plan accompanies the application.

### Heritage

The site is within the Tallowwood Estate Heritage Conservation Area listed in Schedule 5 of the LEP; the dwelling is a neutral building and the land is not a heritage item.

The heritage impact statement by Morrow Heritage Consulting finds that the house is a neutral infill
building and that the addition sits behind the existing ridge, so it is not visible from the street.
The front of the house, its roof and the front garden are unchanged. The design follows council's
pre-DA heritage advice, and the statement concludes that the impact on the conservation area is
acceptable. Council's heritage officer will review the application.

### Hazards

The land is not identified as bush fire prone land on the planning certificate. The land is not flood
affected. The planning certificate records no contamination or acid sulfate soils.

### Waste

Demolition and construction waste will be managed under a site waste management plan, with brick and
metal separated for recycling.

### Construction

The owners will vacate the house during the works. Construction hours will follow council's standard
conditions.

## 6. Notification and determination

Neighbour notification under council's community participation plan; no public exhibition.
Determination under delegation; no local planning panel referral is expected.

## 7. Conclusion

The proposal is a modest single storey rear addition that suits the house and the street. It meets
the LEP standards and the DCP controls, including those for the heritage conservation area, has acceptable impacts on the neighbours, and is recommended
for approval.
