## 1. Introduction

This statement accompanies a development application to the City of Canada Bay for a single-storey
rear addition and internal alterations at 41 Tallowwood Avenue, Concord. It assesses the impact of
the proposal on the heritage significance of the conservation area, following the NSW guidelines for
statements of heritage impact.

The site is within the Tallowwood Estate Heritage Conservation Area listed in Schedule 5 of the LEP.
The dwelling is a neutral building, not a contributory building.

The assessment is based on an inspection of the house and the street on 14 April 2026, council's
conservation area listing and DCP controls, and the DA drawings by Kestrel Hill Architects, including
the proposed ground floor plan A-201.

## 2. The conservation area

The Tallowwood Estate was subdivided in the early 1900s and built out mostly between 1905 and 1925.
Council's statement of significance describes a largely intact Federation suburb: single-storey
brick and timber houses with hipped and gabled roofs, front verandahs, consistent setbacks and open
front gardens. The significance lies chiefly in the streetscape seen from the public domain. Rear
yards are not part of the valued character.

## 3. The house

The house was built in 1958 on a lot left vacant in the original subdivision. It is a single-storey
face brick house with a hipped tiled roof and a simple front porch, typical of its decade. A rear
skillion and laundry lean-to were added in 1987. The house does not share the Federation form,
detailing or materials of its neighbours, and council's DCP map shows it as a neutral building. Its
height, setback and roof form follow the street, so it does not detract from the streetscape, but it
does not contribute to the significance of the area.

## 4. The proposal

- Removal of the 1987 rear skillion and laundry lean-to.
- A single-storey rear addition containing kitchen, living and dining, with a low skillion roof below
  the eave of the main roof.
- A paved terrace and low rear deck.
- Internal alterations, including the bathroom and laundry and the removal of two internal walls.
- No change to the front elevation, the main roof or the front garden.

## 5. Assessment

**Visibility.** The addition sits behind the existing ridge and below the eave line of the main roof.
The house itself screens it from the street, and it cannot be seen from the footpath on either side.

**Form and scale.** The addition is single storey and subordinate to the house. It keeps the side
setbacks of the house and does not change the rhythm of houses and gaps along the street.

**Materials and colours.** Face brick to match the existing house, recessive dark roof sheeting and
muted paint colours are proposed. These follow council's pre-DA advice and do not compete with the
Federation houses nearby.

**Fabric.** The fabric to be removed is either the 1987 additions or internal walls of the neutral
1958 house. None of it has heritage significance.

## 6. Recommendations

1. External colours to be confirmed with council's heritage officer before the construction
   certificate.
2. The front elevation, main roof and front fence to be kept as they are.

## 7. Conclusion

The proposal affects a neutral building and is not visible from the street. It keeps the Federation
streetscape intact, as council's heritage officer asked. The impact on the significance of the
Tallowwood Estate Heritage Conservation Area is acceptable, and the proposal can be supported on
heritage grounds.
