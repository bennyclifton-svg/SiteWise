## Introduction

This statement accompanies a development application to Sutherland Shire Council for a detached
secondary dwelling. The flat sits closer to the rear boundary than the complying development standards
allow; the flat exceeds the CDC rear setback standard, so a development application is lodged rather
than a complying development certificate.

It should be read with the DA drawings by Saltbush Building Design (site plan A-101 Rev B, floor plan,
elevations, sections, roof plan and shadow diagrams), the stormwater plan by Garrick Stormwater
Consulting and the BASIX certificate by Evenstar Energy Ratings.

## The site

The property is 18 Tuckeroo Crescent, Gymea NSW 2227, a regular residential lot of 600 m2 on the
southern side of the street. It holds the owners' family home, which is occupied and will stay occupied
during the works. A driveway along the eastern side of the house leads to the rear yard, which is level
lawn.

The surrounding area is detached family homes on similar lots. The rear neighbour's shed backs onto the
common rear fence.

## The proposal

The proposal is a new single-storey secondary dwelling of 60 m2, detached and free-standing, behind the
occupied main house. The flat has an open kitchen and living area facing north onto a paved courtyard,
two bedrooms and one bathroom with a European laundry. It is built in timber frame on a slab, with
fibre cement cladding, a metal skillion roof and aluminium windows.

A path runs from the street along the driveway to the flat's entry. The flat has no car space; the
occupant parks on the street. Services are taken from the main house: a sub-main from the house
switchboard, water branched after the meter, and drainage to the house drain by gravity. The main house
is not altered.

## Permissibility

The land is in Zone R2 Low Density Residential under Sutherland Shire Local Environmental Plan 2015.
Secondary dwellings are permitted with consent in the R2 Low Density Residential zone.

No clause 4.6 variation is required. The height and floor space ratio standards in the LEP are met, and
the rear setback is a DCP control assessed on its merits.

### Rear setback

The flat is set 1.5 m from the rear boundary. The owners chose this position to keep a larger shared
lawn between the main house and the flat, which gives both dwellings a more useful outdoor area than two
narrow strips either side of a flat set further forward.

The reduced setback has no unreasonable effect on the rear neighbour. The flat is single storey with a
low skillion roof falling to the south. Its rear wall has only small high-level windows, and there is
no overlooking, as the rear neighbour's shed backs onto the fence along this part of the boundary.

## Matters for consideration under section 4.15

### Context and setting

A single-storey flat in a rear yard is common in the street and the wider area. It is not visible from
the street behind the main house.

### Privacy

The living area and courtyard face north towards the main house, not the neighbours. The courtyard is
screened from the main house by a fence and garden planting. Side windows to the west are bedroom
windows with sills set to limit views over the side fence.

### Overshadowing

The shadow diagrams show that in midwinter the flat's shadow falls on the strip of the rear neighbour's
yard behind the fence, mostly on their shed roof. The neighbours' living areas and main private open
space keep their existing sunlight.

### Stormwater

Roof water from the flat is piped by gravity to the existing stormwater line on the lot and on to the
kerb in Tuckeroo Crescent, as shown on the stormwater plan.

### Waste

During construction, waste is kept in a skip on the driveway and removed by the builder. Once occupied,
the flat's bins are stored in a screened area beside the flat and taken to the kerb for council's
collection.

### Energy and water

The BASIX certificate commitments for thermal comfort, energy and water are shown on the DA drawings.
The flat is all-electric with heat-pump hot water.

### Public interest

The proposal adds a modest, well-located home to the area's housing supply without changing the
character of the street.

## Notification and determination

Council will notify the neighbours by letter under its community participation plan; no public
exhibition or community consultation is required. Determination by council staff under delegation; the
application is not referred to the local planning panel.

## Conclusion

The secondary dwelling is permitted with consent, meets the LEP standards and responds to its site. The
1.5 m rear setback is justified by the larger shared lawn and causes no overlooking or unreasonable
overshadowing. We ask that council grant consent.
