# Building Remediation and Rectification Guide

## Purpose and boundary

Use this work-type overlay for rectification of an existing built asset:
facade and water-ingress failures, structural defects, fire-safety upgrades,
services replacement, non-compliant work and systemic building defects.

This guide does not determine causation, liability, code compliance, structural
adequacy, fire safety or fitness for occupation. Those conclusions require the
responsible registered or competent practitioners, certifier, legal advisers,
insurers and authorities. It is platform guidance, not project evidence.

Contaminated land, groundwater and soil remediation are outside this guide.
Load the contaminated-land reference separately when that scope exists. Do not
merge building-defect and contaminated-land risks into one undefined
"remediation" allowance.

## Activation and remediation type

Activate when `work_type: remediation` applies to a residential, commercial or
industrial asset. Classify the primary intervention from evidence:

- investigation and make-safe work;
- facade, roofing, waterproofing or water-ingress rectification;
- structural stabilisation, strengthening or repair;
- passive or active fire-safety rectification;
- services replacement, resilience or compliance upgrade;
- hazardous-building-material removal associated with the works;
- multi-system or whole-building defect rectification.

More than one type may apply. State which are confirmed and which remain
suspected. An observed symptom is not a proven cause.

## Evidence preservation and source hierarchy

Secure and index:

- approved, construction and as-built documents;
- specifications, schedules, substitutions and product records;
- occupation and compliance certificates, fire-safety records and conditions;
- contracts, scopes, warranties, inspection records, test results and notices;
- maintenance, incident, leak, alarm and complaint histories;
- previous reports, opening-up records, photos and repair attempts;
- current surveys, monitoring and laboratory evidence;
- access, occupancy, strata, tenancy and operational constraints.

Record provenance, date, author, revision and whether evidence is observed,
reported, tested or inferred. Preserve samples, photographs and opened
conditions under an agreed protocol if claims or disputes are possible. Legal
advice should govern privilege and expert-evidence arrangements; the project
team must not represent that ordinary project records are privileged.

## Investigation before solution

Develop a staged investigation plan proportionate to risk:

1. define symptoms, affected locations and safety concerns;
2. review records and map recurrence or environmental conditions;
3. perform non-destructive survey where useful;
4. undertake targeted opening-up, sampling, testing and monitoring;
5. form and test causal hypotheses;
6. assess extent, concealed interfaces and recurrence risk;
7. define performance requirements and remediation options;
8. validate the preferred detail through mock-ups or trials where appropriate.

The investigation scope should state access, destructive work, reinstatement,
temporary protection, resident or operator communication, chain of custody,
testing methods and limitations. Do not tender a permanent solution while the
cause or extent remains materially unresolved unless the risk allocation is
explicit and commercially workable.

## Safety, occupation and temporary controls

Identify whether immediate controls are needed before full design:

- exclusion zones, propping, catch platforms or weather protection;
- temporary fire measures or system impairments;
- electrical, water, gas or mechanical isolation;
- mould, dust, asbestos, silica or other exposure controls;
- leak response, monitoring and emergency escalation;
- resident, tenant or operator access restrictions.

Make-safe work is not proof of permanent rectification. Track the owner,
inspection frequency, expiry condition and removal criteria for every temporary
measure.

For occupied buildings, prepare an occupancy interface plan covering notices,
appointments, privacy, safeguarding, accessibility, noise, dust, security,
temporary amenities, business continuity, daily handback and complaints.

## Design basis and option selection

The responsible designers should define:

- the defect mechanism and confidence in the diagnosis;
- existing construction and interfaces to be retained;
- required regulatory and performance outcomes;
- repair extent and treatment of apparently unaffected adjoining work;
- compatible materials, movement, drainage and durability;
- access, sequencing, temporary works and weather constraints;
- inspection, testing and acceptance criteria;
- maintenance needs, design life and residual limitations.

Compare options on safety, recurrence risk, invasiveness, access, programme,
operational impact, maintainability and whole-of-life outcome. Cost must be
computed from project-specific quantities and scope; this guide contains no
rates.

## Governance and approvals

Establish the planning, building-approval, regulated-design, certifier,
fire-safety, heritage, strata and authority pathway from current project advice.
Existing construction is not automatically an approved performance baseline.
Define how the selected work interacts with existing approvals, essential
services, fire-isolated elements and occupation.

Maintain a decision register for:

- accepted diagnosis and its limitations;
- solution and rejected alternatives;
- investigation exclusions and residual risk;
- treatment of consequential damage;
- access and relocation assumptions;
- warranty, insurer and legal dependencies;
- authority and certifier positions.

## Procurement and scope boundaries

Decide whether investigation, design, enabling work and permanent rectification
should be separate packages. Early investigation should not create an
unexamined conflict between the diagnosing consultant and a contractor-led
solution.

Tender documents should distinguish:

- verified quantities from provisional extent;
- opening-up and reinstatement;
- retained, removed and protected work;
- temporary works and occupied-building controls;
- hazardous-material responsibilities;
- samples, prototypes and benchmark areas;
- concealed-condition process and rates or valuation method;
- inspection, test and close-out evidence;
- warranties, access equipment and maintenance information.

## Services by stage

Each stage opens with an **RFP line** that code can render as the lead of a
grouped RFP item. Use only the stages inside the appointment boundary; price
the rest separately or leave them out.

### Inception, site and existing information [inception]

**RFP line:** Review the defect evidence, orders and existing documents, and
preserve the evidence before any work.

- Review defect reports, Building Commission NSW orders or notices, strata
  records, original approvals and as-built information.
- Record and preserve evidence (photographs, samples, chain of custody).
- Identify immediate safety risks and temporary controls needed.

### Concept and schematic design [concept]

**RFP line:** Investigate the cause of the defects and compare remediation
options with cost, disruption and risk.

- Intrusive investigation and testing plan, then findings and cause.
- Options with order-of-cost, programme, occupant impact and residual risk.
- Written client or owners corporation decision on the preferred option.

### Pre-DA consultation and development application [development_application]

**RFP line:** Confirm the approval pathway for the remediation work and
prepare the application documents it needs.

- Exempt, complying development or DA pathway; for Class 2 buildings, the
  Design and Building Practitioners Act 2020 obligations for remedial
  building work.
- Respond to council or certifier queries.

### Conditions of consent and design development [design_development]

**RFP line:** Develop the remediation design to the level the procurement
route requires, coordinated with the other consultants.

- Remediation design with fire, facade, waterproofing and structural input
  as needed.
- Regulated design declarations where the Design and Building
  Practitioners Act 2020 applies.

### Documentation and tender [documentation_tender]

**RFP line:** Prepare the remediation scope and documentation, and support the
tender through queries, addenda and tender review.

- Drawings, specification, scope of works, provisional sums for unknown
  conditions, and the staging and occupancy plan.
- Respond to tenderer RFIs and review tenders.

### Novation and post-contract design [post_contract_design]

**RFP line:** Price, as a separate option, novation or continued design
services to the remediation contractor.

- Review design changes when uncovered conditions differ.

### Construction and completion [construction_completion]

**RFP line:** Inspect the remediation work at hold points, respond to uncovered
conditions, and provide the completion certification.

- Hold-point inspections with photographs before work is concealed.
- Instructions for uncovered conditions and review of variations.
- Completion certification and evidence to close out orders or notices.

### Throughout the appointment [coordination]

**RFP line:** Report to the client or owners corporation and coordinate the
remediation design and consultants throughout the appointment.

- Attend meetings at a stated frequency; keep the defect and evidence
  registers current.

## Consultant RFP minimum returnables

For a building investigator, remedial designer or specialist consultant, seek:

- scope understanding, proposed methodology and limitations;
- relevant defect and building-class experience;
- records review, survey, opening-up, sampling and testing plan;
- safety and occupancy controls for investigations;
- diagnostic report that separates observation, evidence, hypothesis and
  conclusion;
- options assessment and recommended performance requirements;
- coordinated design deliverables and responsibility matrix;
- approvals and regulated-design advice within competence;
- tender support, site inspections, hold points and non-conformance process;
- verification, monitoring and post-completion review;
- team, registrations, insurance, programme, fees, exclusions and variation
  triggers.

Do not ask one consultant to warrant matters outside their competence.

## Construction verification and handover

Use approved samples or benchmark areas to prove access, workmanship and
appearance. Inspection and test plans should include concealed-work hold points,
substrate acceptance, environmental limits, product traceability, photographs,
tests and consultant sign-off appropriate to the design.

At handover collect:

- investigation and design records;
- approved details and departures;
- inspection, test and non-conformance close-out evidence;
- product, batch and installer records where relevant;
- as-built extent maps;
- warranties, care and maintenance requirements;
- monitoring plan and trigger levels;
- resident, operator or facilities-management instructions.

Define a post-completion observation period when performance depends on weather,
seasonal conditions or operation. Do not describe the defect as resolved before
the specified verification evidence is available.

## PMP contribution

The PMP should identify the defect types, evidence status, immediate controls,
investigation stages, design responsibility, approval pathway, occupant and
operator interfaces, procurement strategy, provisional extent, hold points,
verification gates, records protocol and residual uncertainties.

## Common failures and low-confidence flags

Common failures include prescribing a repair from symptoms, insufficient
opening-up, loss of evidence, fragmented responsibility across facade and
waterproofing interfaces, tendering undefined extent as fixed scope, inadequate
temporary protection, poor occupant communication, closing concealed work
before inspection and treating warranty expiry as the technical acceptance
test.

Flag low confidence when approved or as-built records, defect history,
investigation access, cause, extent, hazardous-material information, occupancy
constraints, approval pathway, acceptance criteria or responsible specialist
are missing.
