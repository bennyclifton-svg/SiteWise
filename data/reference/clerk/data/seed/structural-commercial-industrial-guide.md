# Structural Engineering: Commercial and Industrial

## Purpose and professional boundary

The structural engineer designs and certifies the structure: footings,
retaining, slabs, frames, lateral stability, and the structural elements of
the facade, roof and plant supports. The engineer relies on the geotechnical
engineer for ground conditions, the architect for geometry, and the services
engineers for loads and penetrations. Clerk frames the scope, deliverables and
interfaces; it does not size members or judge structural adequacy.

## Activation

Use this guide for Class 3 and 5 to 9 buildings, industrial warehouses and
sheds, mixed-use podiums, and structural work on existing commercial
buildings. Use `structural-residential.md` for Class 1 and 10 work.

## Evidence sweep

- Geotechnical report: site classification, bearing capacity, groundwater,
  rock levels, earthquake site sub-soil class and retaining parameters.
- Survey, architectural drawings, services loads and plant weights.
- For existing buildings: original structural drawings, certification,
  condition and any concrete cancer, corrosion or overloading history.
- Adjoining structures, excavation depths and the need for dilapidation
  surveys and shoring.

## Services by stage

Each stage opens with an **RFP line** that code can render as the lead of a
grouped RFP item. Stage ids match `architectural-services-guide.md`. Use only
the stages inside the appointment boundary; price the rest separately or
leave them out.

### Inception, site and existing information [inception]

**RFP line:** Review the geotechnical report, the site, adjoining structures
and any existing structure, and set the structural design basis.

- Site visit; review the geotechnical report and identify gaps (for example
  no groundwater data or no rock level near deep excavation).
- For existing buildings, inspect the structure and identify testing needed
  (cover meter, cores, load testing).
- Propose the design basis: importance level, design life, loads (AS/NZS
  1170 series), and earthquake and wind parameters.

### Concept and schematic design [concept]

**RFP line:** Compare structural systems and provide grid, depth and
transfer advice for the schematic design.

- Structural system options (post-tensioned, conventional reinforced,
  precast, steel portal, tilt-up) with cost and programme implications.
- Structural zones, column grid, transfer levels and core locations.
- Basement retention and dewatering strategy with the geotechnical engineer.

### Pre-DA consultation and development application [development_application]

**RFP line:** Provide the structural inputs the development application needs
and respond to council requests on them.

- Structural feasibility statement, excavation and shoring concept near
  boundaries, and any council-required adjoining-property statements.

### Conditions of consent and design development [design_development]

**RFP line:** Review the structural conditions of consent and develop the
structural design, coordinated with the consultant team.

- Conditions affecting structure (dilapidation surveys, excavation support,
  vibration monitoring).
- Develop framing, lateral system, footings, retaining and slab design;
  coordinate penetrations and plant loads with services engineers.
- Fire resistance levels of structural members with the BCA consultant and
  fire engineer.

### Documentation and tender [documentation_tender]

**RFP line:** Prepare structural documentation and certification for the
construction certificate and tender, and answer tender queries.

- Structural drawings, specification and design certificate for the
  construction certificate.
- Design and construct route: state which elements (for example precast
  panels, steel connections, post-tensioning) the contractor designs.
- Respond to tenderer RFIs and review structural alternatives offered.

### Novation and post-contract design [post_contract_design]

**RFP line:** Price, as a separate option, novation to the design and
construct contractor and the post-contract structural design.

- Novation terms and fee basis; shop drawing review for precast, steel,
  post-tensioning and temporary works interfaces.

### Construction and completion [construction_completion]

**RFP line:** Inspect the structure at hold points, review shop drawings and
provide the structural certification for the occupation certificate.

- Hold-point inspections: footings, reinforcement before each pour,
  post-tensioning, steel connections and retaining walls.
- Instructions where ground or existing conditions differ from the design
  basis.
- Structural completion certificate and as-built review.

### Throughout the appointment [coordination]

**RFP line:** Attend design meetings, coordinate the structural design with
the other disciplines and report on design status throughout the
appointment.

- Attend meetings at a stated frequency; keep a register of inspections,
  certificates and design changes.

## Consultant RFP minimum returnables

- Lump sum fee by stage, hourly rates and an allowance for additional
  inspections.
- Number of site inspections included, and the hold points they cover.
- Stated reliance on the geotechnical report and what happens if it changes.
- Exclusions (temporary works design, shoring design, facade engineering).
- Professional indemnity insurance level and registration (NSW Design and
  Building Practitioners Act 2020 where it applies; RPEQ for Queensland).

## Common failures and low-confidence flags

- Geotechnical report is too early or too shallow for the final design.
- Temporary works, shoring and crane loads fall between engineer and builder.
- Inspection count too low for the number of pours or stages.
- Contractor-designed elements not listed, so certification has gaps.
- Flag low confidence when the existing structure has no drawings or when
  excavation is within the zone of influence of adjoining footings.
