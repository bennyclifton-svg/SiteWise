# Architectural Services Guide

## Purpose and professional boundary

Use this guide to scope, procure and manage the client's architect: the RFP
for architectural services, the consultant services agreement, fee review,
design review and the handover of design responsibility to a contractor.

This guide is platform guidance, not project evidence. It does not design,
certify compliance, assess a development application, or give planning, legal
or registration advice. In NSW, only a person registered under the
Architects Act 2003 may use the title "architect". Registration, insurance,
Design and Building Practitioners Act 2020 duties, planning pathways and
certifier requirements change. Verify current NSW Architects Registration
Board, NSW Planning Portal, council and Building Commission NSW requirements
before relying on any statement here.

The architect's scope is set by the **appointment boundary** (the stages the
client buys) and by the **procurement route** (who completes the design after
the tender). Most scope errors in architect RFPs come from getting one of
these wrong, not from missing a technical item.

## Activation

Activate this guide when the project:

- appoints, or is about to appoint, an architect or building designer;
- needs a planning approval (DA, modification, complying development
  certificate or state significant development) prepared by a design team;
- is procured through design and construct, with novation or a
  contractor-led design after the tender;
- needs a design review, fee proposal review or architect variation
  assessment.

Do not activate the whole guide for a minor architectural input to another
discipline's work. Match scope to evidence and risk.

## Architectural evidence sweep

Seek:

- the client brief or project proposal (functional, operational, commercial
  and quality objectives), with its revision and date;
- a site identification survey (boundaries, levels, services, trees, adjoining
  buildings), title search, easements, covenants and rights of carriageway;
- planning information: zoning, LEP and DCP controls, applicable SEPPs,
  planning certificate, heritage, flooding, bushfire, contamination and any
  planning due diligence report;
- existing drawings, services records, fire safety schedules and condition
  reports for existing buildings or infrastructure on or under the site;
- the project programme, the procurement strategy and the budget or cost plan;
- other consultants' appointments and scopes, to fix interfaces and avoid
  duplication;
- the client's consultancy agreement and any required departures.

Record what was **not** available. An RFP that relies on attachments (survey,
due diligence, concept sketches) must list them. If they are missing, it
states the assumption instead of inventing site facts.

## Appointment boundary and procurement route

Decide these before drafting services, because they decide which stages
below are in scope:

| Route | Client architect's usual boundary | Later design by |
|---|---|---|
| Traditional (lump sum, fully documented) | Inception through construction and defects liability | The client's architect throughout |
| Design and construct, novation | Inception to DA approval or to tender documentation; then novated | The same architect, engaged by the contractor after novation |
| Design and construct, no novation | Inception to DA and D&C tender documentation | The contractor's own design team |
| Early contractor involvement | Inception to developed design, with a contractor in a parallel pre-construction stage | Varies; state it |
| Planning-only appointment | Inception to DA determination | Separate appointment |

State the boundary in the RFP. Services beyond the boundary may still be
priced as **separate options** (for example novation or construction-phase
services), but they must not be presented as firm scope.

## Services by stage

Each stage below opens with an **RFP line**: one canonical sentence that code
can render as the lead of a grouped RFP item. The bullets are the services an
RFP should normally request in that stage. Stage ids are in brackets and are
the catalog `stage` values. Use only the stages within the appointment
boundary. Price the rest separately or leave them out.

### Inception, site and existing information [inception]

**RFP line:** Establish the project basis: visit the site, review the brief
and all existing information, and confirm the planning controls and missing
inputs.

- Attend a site visit and assess existing site conditions, access, adjoining
  properties and existing buildings or infrastructure.
- Review existing information: survey, title and easements, planning and
  technical due diligence reports, existing drawings, services records and
  fire safety schedules.
- Review the client brief and provide written input: gaps, conflicts, area
  and budget pressures, and decisions needed.
- Review the planning controls (SEPPs, LEP, DCP) with the town planner, and
  identify the likely approval pathway and key constraints.
- Identify the further investigations and consultant inputs needed, with
  their programme dependencies.
- Deliverables: site and existing-information summary, brief review notes,
  planning constraints summary, and a list of required inputs.

### Concept and schematic design [concept]

**RFP line:** Prepare concept and schematic design options for client review,
and develop the approved option into a coordinated schematic design.

- Prepare concept options (site planning, massing, access and yield or area
  testing) for client review and a documented decision.
- Develop the approved option into schematic design: plans, sections,
  elevations, area schedule and outline materials.
- Coordinate early consultant inputs that shape the scheme (for example
  traffic and vehicle access, civil and stormwater, structure, fire strategy
  and sustainability targets).
- Provide design information for the cost planner's estimate, and respond to
  cost advice.
- Obtain written client approval of the schematic design before DA work
  starts.

### Pre-DA consultation and development application [development_application]

**RFP line:** Consult council before lodgement, then prepare and coordinate
the development application package and support it through assessment to
determination.

- Prepare the pre-DA meeting package, attend the meeting with council (and
  any design review panel) and incorporate the feedback into the design.
- Prepare the architectural DA drawings and reports, and coordinate the
  specialist consultants' reports for a complete lodgement package.
- Support the town planner to lodge on the NSW Planning Portal, and respond to
  completeness checks.
- During assessment, respond to council requests for information, attend
  meetings and amend drawings until determination. State whether this is in
  the lump sum or charged at hourly rates, with an allowance.
- For residential apartment buildings, provide the design verification
  statement and design quality responses the planning instruments require.
- Deliverables: pre-DA package and minutes, DA drawing set and architectural
  reports, and an RFI response register.

### Conditions of consent and design development [design_development]

**RFP line:** Review the conditions of consent and develop the approved
design, coordinating the consultant team, to the level the procurement route
requires.

- Review the conditions of consent, identify those that affect the design,
  documentation, cost or programme, and advise on any modification.
- Develop the design with the consultant team: structure, services, fire,
  BCA and access, sustainability and NCC energy efficiency (Section J), and
  civil and external works interfaces.
- Maintain a design responsibility matrix and coordinate the design between
  disciplines.
- Update the area schedule and provide information for the cost plan update.

### Documentation and tender [documentation_tender]

**RFP line:** Prepare the architectural documentation for the chosen
procurement route and support the tender through queries, addenda and tender
review.

- Traditional route: prepare construction certificate and contract
  documentation, including the architectural specification and schedules.
- Design and construct route: prepare design intent drawings, the design
  brief or performance requirements, and state the design responsibility the
  contractor takes over.
- Coordinate the documentation for compliance with the conditions of consent
  and the BCA consultant's advice.
- During the tender, respond to tenderer RFIs, prepare addenda, and review
  tender clarifications and design departures.
- Deliverables: tender drawing and document set, RFI and addenda register,
  and design input into the tender recommendation.

### Novation and post-contract design [post_contract_design]

**RFP line:** Price, as a separate option, novation to the design and
construct contractor and the post-contract design and documentation.

- Novation terms: timing, the deed, how duty-of-care and design
  responsibility pass, and the fee basis after novation.
- Post-contract design development and construction documentation for the
  contractor, shop drawing review and construction-stage design queries.
- Only include this stage when the procurement strategy uses novation.
  Otherwise, ask for hourly rates for any post-tender design advice.

### Construction and completion [construction_completion]

**RFP line:** Provide construction-phase services as the client's architect:
site inspections, submittal and RFI responses, contract administration where
engaged, and completion and defects support.

- Traditional route only: contract administration or Superintendent services
  if engaged, site inspections, instructions, variation assessment, progress
  claim review and practical completion inspections.
- Shop drawing and submittal review, RFI responses and design clarifications.
- Defects inspections during the defects liability period, and any design
  compliance declarations the regulatory regime requires.
- For design and construct appointments that end at DA or tender, list this
  stage as excluded or as a separately priced option, never as firm scope.

### Throughout the appointment [coordination]

**RFP line:** Lead and coordinate the design across the consultant team
throughout the appointment, with regular client meetings, reporting and
design management.

- Attend client and design team meetings at a stated frequency, and issue
  minutes and action lists.
- Keep a deliverables schedule, design query register and document register.
- Report the design status, risks and decisions required against the
  programme.
- Manage design changes: record the instruction, cost and programme effect,
  and the client's approval.

## Conditional services

Request these only when the evidence triggers them, and attach them to the
stage where they occur:

- Heritage impact input: the site is listed or in a conservation area.
- Fire engineering and performance solution coordination: the brief or
  authorities indicate a performance solution, fire brigade access issues or
  smoke clearance.
- Building over or next to existing infrastructure (detention systems,
  easements, services corridors): coordinate with the civil engineer.
- Easements, rights of carriageway and shared accessways: design the boundary
  treatments, gates and access control they require.
- Existing buildings that stay occupied: interfaces, staging and temporary
  arrangements.
- Specialist facilities (industrial logistics, cold chain, health, education,
  laboratory): coordinate the operational brief and specialist consultants.
- Interior design, tenant fit-out and marketing material (renders,
  perspectives): only when the brief asks for them. They are usually excluded
  on speculative base-building projects.

## Consultant RFP minimum returnables

Request:

- scope understanding, the information relied on, and exclusions stated item
  by item;
- deliverables by stage;
- a programme response against the project programme, with resource hours per
  team member for each stage;
- the team, registrations, relevant experience with this building type, and
  insurances (professional indemnity and public liability);
- acceptance of, or itemised departures from, the client's consultancy
  services agreement;
- a lump-sum fee by stage within the appointment boundary, hourly rates,
  reimbursables and variation triggers, with each option priced separately
  (DA assessment amendments, novation, construction phase);
- quality, WHS and environmental management systems.

## Fee stages

Align the fee table with the appointment boundary. Use the stage ids above:
Inception and schematic design, Development application, Design development,
Documentation and tender. List Novation or post-contract design, and
Construction and completion, **only** when they are in scope or priced as
options. A generic fee table that includes construction-phase and handover
stages invites padding on a design and construct appointment that ends at DA
or tender.

## Architectural design review

At each stage gate, check:

- the brief: every brief requirement is addressed, or its departure is
  recorded and approved;
- planning: the design responds to the controls and to pre-DA feedback, and
  to the conditions of consent after determination;
- coordination: consultant inputs are incorporated, and the design
  responsibility matrix is current;
- buildability and cost: the cost plan reflects the current design, and
  departures from budget are reported;
- compliance inputs: BCA, access, fire and energy-efficiency advice is
  reflected, or its gaps are recorded;
- document control: the revision, status and issue purpose are clear on every
  sheet.

## PMP contribution

State the appointment boundary, the procurement route, the stages and
deliverables in scope, the approval pathway, the design responsibility
handover (novation or none), key consultant interfaces, design stage gates
and client decision dates, and unresolved assumptions.

## Common failure modes

- An RFP that omits the stage backbone (site visit, existing-information
  review, options for client approval, pre-DA meeting, DA conditions) and
  lists only project-specific technical items.
- Construction-phase, commissioning or handover services requested from an
  architect whose appointment ends at DA or tender.
- DA assessment support left unstated. Proposals then exclude council
  amendments or charge them hourly without an allowance.
- The Section J / ESD, fire engineering and civil external-works boundaries
  left unstated, so they fall between the consultants.
- Novation neither requested as an option nor excluded, which leaves design
  responsibility after the tender unclear.
- A specification requirement left unclear on a design and construct tender.
- Tenant fit-out or marketing renders requested on a speculative base
  building.
- A fee table that does not match the appointment boundary.

## Low-confidence and escalation flags

Flag low confidence when the survey, planning controls, due diligence,
existing-building records, procurement strategy or programme are missing.
Escalate to the project lead when:

- the procurement route or appointment boundary is undecided;
- the brief conflicts with planning controls or the budget;
- a performance solution, heritage listing, contamination or easement could
  change the approval pathway;
- a tenderer departs from the consultancy agreement on liability, insurance,
  novation or intellectual property.
